- When composing pull request descriptions, load and use the `explaining-code` skill. Explain the change, not just the edits: what happened before, what happens now, and why. Lead with a short explanation of the changed behavior in plain technical prose.
- Use words and diagrams together as the default for non-trivial changes. Include a focused diagram that helps the reviewer understand the change, such as a before/after flow, a sequence of interactions, or a shift in responsibility between components. Show what changed, not just the surrounding architecture, and use prose to explain the intent and consequences rather than narrating every box and arrow.
- Prefer a concise explanation paired with a diagram over a wall of text or a file-by-file change list. For trivial changes where a diagram would add no useful information, keep the description short and prose-only; do not add decorative diagrams.
